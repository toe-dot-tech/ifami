How ifami works

The bar on each row

The bar is the file, drawn to scale. The shaded part is what is already saved on your disk. The bright line on the right is the download happening right now.

When a connection breaks, ifami notes the exact byte it stopped at and carries on from there, so the marks you see on the bar are where your connection was unreliable.

If the site never says how big the file is, the bar is drawn dashed and labelled “size unknown” rather than guessing.

Doing things

Every download has three buttons on the right:

❙❙
pause, or resume if paused
≡
open details and the activity log
×
remove it, keeping the finished file

“Add download” at the top takes a link, and the tabs below it narrow the list to what you care about right now. Everything else you can do to a download is on the row itself.

Your privacy

ifami has no account, no sign-in, and no advertising. It has no analytics of any kind, and it never asks for an identifier — not a user id, not a device fingerprint. The only network traffic is the download itself, sent to the server you asked for.

The clipboard

ifami offers you a link the moment you copy it, because that is how a link gets from your browser to a desktop app. While this window is focused it looks at your clipboard to ask one question: is this a link? If it is not, the text is thrown away unread. Nothing is stored, and it does not watch while the window is in the background.

The little icon next to the link is fetched from the site you are downloading from — never from an icon-lookup service, which would mean telling a stranger every site you visit. Sites with no icon just show a letter.
